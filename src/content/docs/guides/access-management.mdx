Invite people and share only the workspaces they need. Ask a tenant Admin or Owner to handle invitations and tenant-wide membership. A tenant is the account area for a person or organization; workspaces separate the information stored within it.

## Invite a collaborator

As a tenant Admin or Owner, you can ask your connected assistant:

> Invite this email address as a Member. Show me the recipient, role, and expiry before creating the invitation.

1. Receive the invitation token created with `create_invite`. Only an Owner can invite another Owner.
2. Deliver the token privately. Invitation emails are not sent automatically.
3. The recipient signs up with the invitation, or authenticates with an existing account and uses `accept_invite`. The account's persisted email must match the recipient. Service credentials cannot accept invitations.
4. Grant a Member or Viewer access to the intended workspace as well. Joining the tenant alone does not open a workspace.

Accepting an invitation does not issue a login credential or switch the connected workspace. Use the normal authentication flow for sign-in and signup. A mutable, unverified email alone is not proof of identity.

Check invitations with `list_invites`. Use `revoke_invite` to cancel an unused invitation, or `reissue_invite` with a new expiry to replace its token. Reissuing invalidates the old token. Deliver the new token yourself; listings do not show tokens.

## Roles and effective access

An action must be allowed by both your role and your API key. A more powerful key cannot grant an action that your role does not permit.

| Tenant role   | Maximum access                                                   | Workspace membership                          |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Owner / Admin | Content operations up to `migration`, plus tenant administration | Inherited administration of tenant workspaces |
| Member        | Content operations up to `write`                                 | A direct workspace grant is required          |
| Viewer        | Content operations up to `read`                                  | A direct workspace grant is required          |

Direct workspace roles are Admin, Member, and Viewer, with content ceilings of `migration`, `write`, and `read`. The tenant-role ceiling still applies. For example, a tenant Member who becomes a workspace Admin remains limited to `write` content operations. They can manage direct workspace grants but do not gain `schema` access. Team-based grants are not supported.

> Show the people with direct access to this workspace, then grant the selected member Viewer access. Show me the person and workspace before changing anything.

Workspace Admins use `list_workspace_members`, `grant_workspace_member`, and `revoke_workspace_member` to manage individual grants. Recipients must already belong to the tenant. The list shows direct grants only, not inherited access from tenant Owner/Admin roles.

Workspace Admin alone does not grant invitations, tenant membership administration, key issuance, ownership transfer, or billing powers. Revoking a direct grant also revokes affected workspace-bound keys when access is not inherited. It does not remove tenant membership.

A promotion does not widen the scope of an existing key. Audit records require a separate `audit` grant, even for administrators. Service credentials have no attributed human role and cannot act as a human administrator or change a personal profile. Their scope, binding, expiry, and the tenant's active status still apply.

## Change membership and ownership

> Before changing this person's role, show me which workspaces and keys would be affected. Do not make the change yet.

Use `preview_member_role_change` or `preview_member_removal` before requesting a change. A preview describes the situation when checked, not permission for a later action.

Use `list_members` and `set_member_role` to add an existing account or change a role. Invite people who do not have an account. Removing membership with `remove_member` also revokes that person's attributed tenant keys, but keeps their shared account for other tenants.

Only an Owner can add or remove Owner authority. The last Owner cannot be removed or demoted. To hand over ownership, check an existing member's UUID and use `transfer_ownership`. The caller becomes an Admin as part of the transfer.

## Tenant and workspace administration

> Show the name and UUID of the connected tenant and workspace. Do not change anything.

Matching names can refer to different places. Check UUIDs with `whoami` before requesting administration.

| What you want to do                            | Tools                                                                      | Who can do it and what to check                                                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Change the tenant name or manual workspace cap | `get_tenant`, `update_tenant`                                              | Tenant Admin/Owner; plan quotas do not change                                                             |
| Create, inspect, or delete a workspace         | `create_workspace`, `list_workspaces`, `get_workspace`, `delete_workspace` | Creation/deletion require tenant Admin/Owner; deletion removes data and the last workspace is protected   |
| Rename a workspace                             | `update_workspace`                                                         | Workspace Admin; schema binding does not change                                                           |
| Change a manual entity cap                     | `update_workspace`                                                         | Tenant Admin/Owner required                                                                               |
| Change your display name                       | `get_current_user`, `update_current_user`                                  | An attributed user key is required; the name is shared across tenants; email/password/OAuth do not change |

To change a manual cap, supply `replace_max_workspaces=true` with `max_workspaces` for a tenant, or `replace_max_entities=true` with `max_entities` for a workspace. Set the value to `null` to clear it. Omitting the field does not clear it, and plan quotas still apply separately.

Selected-workspace detail, update, and schema operations cannot switch a key's binding. Direct-grant administration and workspace deletion take an explicit same-tenant target and check permission for that operation. This does not make the key generally retargetable.

## Management boundaries

The tools above cover ordinary collaboration. These operations work differently:

- Tenant creation, server-wide tenant inventory, and physical deletion require the server operator's CLI, not a tenant-bound key. See [Tenant retirement](../../operations/tenant-retirement/).
- Ask the operator for server-wide user inventory or account deletion. This is separate from leaving a tenant.
- Archiving a schema does not physically delete its data. The shared template library is also separate from physical PostgreSQL schemas. See [Templates](../templates/#schema-versions-and-archival).
- Not every REST action is available through MCP. Marketplace publishing, inference job execution, sign-in/OAuth, Stripe webhooks, streaming export, and server maintenance are outside management MCP coverage. Provider configuration neither runs inference nor removes licence restrictions.

After access is revoked, already-authenticated requests may finish. Queued work rechecks permission before writes/publication, but not every external call already in progress can be stopped. See [API keys](../api-keys/) for replacing credentials.
